TITLE: CYRTOSPERMA GRESSITTIORUM
AUTHORITY: A.Hay
TAGS: Cyrtosperma, Oceania, New Guinea, Papua New Guinea, Milne Bay
PHOTOS: no photograph of a living plant in the pack; Hay 1988 Fig. 4 (habit, leaf and flowers, from the type); type sheets L 0041894, A 00025751

PROTOLOGUE:
Cyrtosperma gressittiorum A.Hay, published in Blumea 33: 439 (1988), as "gressittorum", with Fig. 4.
Type: L.J. Brass 28049, Papua New Guinea, Milne Bay Province, Sudest (Tagula) Island, Rambuso, 11 September 1956 (holotype L; isotype A). Hay's diagnosis: differs from all other species of Cyrtosperma in the hooded spathe.²

SYNONYMS:
HOMOTYPIC SYNONYMS: N/A
HETEROTYPIC SYNONYMS: N/A
ACCEPTED INFRASPECIFICS: N/A

DISTRIBUTION:
New Guinea: New Guinea (Sudest Island).¹
DISTRIBUTION NOTE: Known only from the type gathering on Sudest (Tagula) Island in the Louisiade Archipelago, in rainforest on the crest of a ridge at 150 m.²

SPECIES DESCRIPTION:
Herb to 70 cm tall. Petiole to 60 cm long, the sheath papery and c. 15 cm long, the spines sparse, straight, needle-like, to 3 mm long; petiole variegated green and white. Blade hastate-sagittate, sparsely armed below, c. 30 cm long; anterior lobe subequalling the posterior ones; posterior lobes slender, to 6 cm wide, their midribs naked in the sinus for c. 3 cm, the lesser venation somewhat prominent below; costae of the anterior lobe arising near the sinus, curvinerved, running to the margin near the tip.²

INFLORESCENCE:
Peduncle similar to but exceeding the petiole, to 90 cm long. Spathe lanceolate, c. 7 cm long, hood-forming in the upper quarter, green, the major venation parallel and distant, the secondary venation reticulate; spadix orange, 4 cm long, with a 5 mm stipe adnate to the spathe. Flowers tetramerous; anthers exserted from the tepals at male anthesis and apparently retracting afterwards; stigmas hardly exceeding the tepals; ovule solitary. Fruit and seed unknown.²

ECOLOGY:
Ridge-crest rainforest at 150 m.²

DISTRIBUTION MAPS:
Hay 1988 Fig. 3.

ETYMOLOGY:
Commemorates the entomologist and biogeographer J. Linsley Gressitt and his wife Margaret, who died together in an air crash in China in April 1982; it was through Gressitt, at the Wau Ecology Institute, that Hay was first introduced to tropical vegetation. Hay spelt the epithet gressittorum; POWO and the World Checklist correct it to gressittiorum, the genitive plural for two people, and IPNI keeps the original spelling.² ¹

NOTES:
1. The hooded (cucullate) spathe is unique in the genus, and the only species with a green spathe and an orange spadix.²
2. The second Louisiade endemic, with C. brassii of Rossel Island; both are tetramerous members of the Merkusii group.²
3. Still known from the one collection of 1956 as far as the sources consulted show.⁴ ⚠

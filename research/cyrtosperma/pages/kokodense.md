TITLE: CYRTOSPERMA KOKODENSE
AUTHORITY: A.Hay
TAGS: Cyrtosperma, Oceania, New Guinea, Papua New Guinea, Oro, Central Province
PHOTOS: no photograph of a living plant in the pack; Hay 1988 Fig. 9 (from NGF 23585 and Cheesman 3); type sheets L 0041895, L 0041896, G 00191357

PROTOLOGUE:
Cyrtosperma kokodense A.Hay, published in Blumea 33: 447 (1988), with Fig. 9.
Type: C.E. Carr 16194, Papua New Guinea, Northern (Oro) Province, Kokoda, 22 March 1936 (holotype L; isotypes B, BM, CANB, SING). Hay's diagnosis: differs from C. cuspidispathum in the mostly straight spines, the armed blade, the spathe not convolute at the base and not long-acuminate at the apex.²

DISTRIBUTION:
New Guinea: New Guinea.¹
DISTRIBUTION NOTE: Northern (Oro) and Central Provinces, Papua New Guinea: Kokoda, the Kokoda Trail and Boridi village.² Records 1933–1973, at 350–1,190 m.⁴

SPECIES DESCRIPTION:
Herb to 1.5 m tall. Blade markedly hastate to sagittate, c. 40–100 cm long in flowering plants; anterior lobe to 30 cm from tip to sinus; posterior lobes obliquely narrowly ovate, to c. 75 cm long, with rather fine and numerous costae diverging from the midribs at about 35 degrees. Petioles sometimes mottled; armature sparse, short, slender, to 4 mm long, straight to upcurved.²

INFLORESCENCE:
Spathe to 25 cm long, lanceolate, to 2.5 cm wide, in the lower 5 cm the margins clasping the spadix but not overlapping, deflected in the middle part, spirally twisted in the upper part, the tip not or hardly acuminate, the whole purple, paler within and with yellowish streaks; spadix stipitate for 1 cm, 4.5–5.5 cm long and c. 6 mm wide in flower, cylindrical. Flowers hexamerous; anthers not exceeding the tepals at male anthesis; stigmas brown when dried, papillate; ovary 1(–2)-ovulate. Ripe fruit and seed unknown; Hay expected the fruits to be expelled from the spadix as in C. cuspidispathum.²

ECOLOGY:
Rainforest undergrowth and stream sides at around 400 m.²

DISTRIBUTION MAPS:
Hay 1988 Fig. 8.

ETYMOLOGY:
Of Kokoda, the type locality in Oro Province, at the northern end of the Kokoda Trail.

NOTES:
1. The second member of the Cuspidispathum group: stamens not exserted, spathe twisted above; it is the smaller plant with straight spines and an armed blade.²
2. Named from material gathered by C.E. Carr in 1936 and by Evelyn Cheesman, who also collected at Kokoda.²

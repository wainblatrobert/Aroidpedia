DISTRIBUTION:
New Guinea: New Guinea (south-west).¹
DISTRIBUTION NOTE: South-western New Guinea; iNaturalist records are from Indonesian Papua.³ Living plants are grown at the Bogor Botanic Gardens, where a Sorong collection long labelled with this name proved to be C. prasinispathum.⁵

SPECIES DESCRIPTION:
⚠ To be taken from the protologue. What the sources in hand show: a plant close enough to C. prasinispathum to have been confused with it at Bogor; a recent figure describes its characteristic "rabbit-ear" leaves.⁵ ⁶

ETYMOLOGY:
Presumably for Gregori Garnadi Hambali, the Indonesian aroid specialist at Bogor; ⚠ to be confirmed from the protologue.

NOTES:
1. Hariri et al. (2024) compare their new C. prasinispathum with the Bogor plants that had carried this name and figure a leaf of C. hambalii beside it (their Fig. 1J).⁵
2. Six wild observations on iNaturalist, four of them research grade, are currently the best photographs of the species available to the build.³

TITLE: CYRTOSPERMA BRASSII
AUTHORITY: A.Hay
TAGS: Cyrtosperma, Oceania, New Guinea, Papua New Guinea, Milne Bay
PHOTOS: no photograph of a living plant in the pack; Hay 1988 Fig. 5 (from a plant cultivated at Lae); type sheets L 0041891, A 00025750

PROTOLOGUE:
Cyrtosperma brassii A.Hay, published in Blumea 33: 439 (1988), with Fig. 5.
Type: L.J. Brass 28282, Papua New Guinea, Milne Bay Province, Rossel Island, Abaleti, 1 October 1956 (holotype L; isotypes A, LAE). Hay's diagnosis: differs from C. merkusii in the tetramerous flowers, the smooth seeds and the leaves that are rough to the touch when dried.²

SYNONYMS:
HOMOTYPIC SYNONYMS: N/A
HETEROTYPIC SYNONYMS: N/A
ACCEPTED INFRASPECIFICS: N/A

DISTRIBUTION:
New Guinea: New Guinea (Rossel Island).¹
DISTRIBUTION NOTE: Confined to Rossel Island at the eastern end of the Louisiade Archipelago; besides the type, Brass 28251, NGF 27090 and a plant grown at Lae.² Records from 1956–1982, at 50 m.⁴

SPECIES DESCRIPTION:
Robust herb to 2.5 m tall. Leaves 2 to several; petioles spreading out from the base, very prickly with small spines arranged in combs at least towards the base, mottled green and white; blade sagittate, to 120 cm long, held with the anterior lobe up and the posterior lobes down; costae of the anterior lobe pinnate, spreading; posterior lobes with the midribs naked in the sinus for c. 6 cm, to 30 cm wide at the widest point, with acuminate tips; both surfaces of the blade rough (asperous) in the dry state though not when fresh.²

INFLORESCENCE:
Peduncle similar to but usually somewhat shorter than the petioles. Spathe ovate-lanceolate, to 18 cm long, remaining somewhat constricted above the level of the spadix apex after opening, boat-shaped, blackish purple, with parallel major venation and reticulate secondary venation; spadix with a c. 5 mm free, unarmed stipe, to c. 7 cm long, creamy yellow. Flowers tetramerous; ovaries 1(–2)-ovulate; anthers exserted from the tepals at male anthesis. Fruit ovoid, orange-red. Seed smooth, very faintly longitudinally ridged.²

ECOLOGY:
Rainforest on Rossel Island, at low altitude.² ⁴

DISTRIBUTION MAPS:
Hay 1988 Fig. 3.

ETYMOLOGY:
For Leonard J. Brass (1900–1971), the Australian botanist of the Archbold Expeditions who collected the type on the Fifth Archbold Expedition in 1956.

NOTES:
1. One of two Louisiade endemics, with C. gressittiorum on Sudest; both are tetramerous members of the Merkusii group, the group otherwise hexamerous.² With C. bougainvillense they are the closest relatives of the giant swamp taro, which bears on the question of where that crop came into cultivation.²
2. Grown at the Lae Botanic Gardens in the 1980s, from which Hay drew the figure.²

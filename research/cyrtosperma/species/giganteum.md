# Cyrtosperma giganteum Engl.

**Status:** accepted (POWO/WCVP).
**Protologue:** Engler, A. (1910). Araceae. Nova Guinea 8(1): 249; also Pflanzenreich IV.23C: 17 (1911).
**Type:** Versteeg 1818, Irian Jaya [Papua], Lorentz (Noord) River, flowering October 1907 (BO, lectotype, selected by Hay 1988); Versteeg 1141 (May 1907) is the other original collection. Von Römer 193, cited by Engler, was not located by Hay.
**Informal group:** tentatively Merkusii group (Hay 1988) on its exserted anthers and crested seeds.
**IDs:** IPNI 86818-1 · POWO urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:86818-1 · WCVP 54881 · WFO wfo-0000936214? (see names.json) · GBIF (see CSV) · iNat taxon 937778.

## Original description (Engler 1910: 249, held; Hay's translation 1988: 442)

"Leaf with petiole to 2.5 m long, adult blade coriaceous, ovate-sagittate,
anterior lobe to about 75 cm long, 70 cm wide at the base, obtuse or apiculate
at the apex, posterior lobes to 1 m or more long, 50 cm wide, bent back,
subacute, separated by a deep acute sinus, with about 8 distant lateral
primary nerves joined into a marginal collective vein, posterior costae naked
in the sinus for about 10 cm. Peduncle to 4.5 cm diam., smooth. Spathe
ovate-lanceolate bearing a long acumen, reddish-brown, convolute below, c. 8
cm diam., c. 35 cm or more long; spadix dark rose, stipitate for 2 cm, 21 cm
long, about 4 cm thick. Stamens 4–5, filaments to 1 mm long. Pistil 4 mm
long, ovary pluriovulate. Perianth with 4–5 tepals 3.5 mm long. Fruiting
spadix to 36 cm long, 9 cm thick. Berries ellipsoid, green, 1.5 cm long, 6 mm
thick. Seeds several." Locality: Dutch New Guinea, on the Noord River
(Versteeg 1141, May 1907; 1818, flowering October 1907).

## What else is known (Hay 1988: 442–443)

"This huge plant is poorly known." Spirit material at Bogor and a sterile
leaf at Leiden show the blade and upper petiole unarmed, the lower petiole
with scattered straight spines. Anthers are exserted in a preserved fruiting
spadix and the seeds are longitudinally crested like those of *C. merkusii*.
The infructescence holds some 1,300 fruits, each with up to five seeds — by
far the largest in the genus; the pluriovulate ovary led Engler to ally it
with African *Lasiomorpha*, but its marcescent spathe, geniculate petiole and
peduncle and free filaments place it in *Cyrtosperma* proper. Known to Hay
only from the Lorentz River collections (Versteeg 1141, 1818).

## Since 1988: Hay & Imran (2020), Aroideana 43(3–4): 4–11

"*Cyrtosperma giganteum* Engl. (Araceae–Lasioideae) found again in Papua
Province, Indonesia" — read 2026-09-07 from the Drive text export
(`LITERATURE`, Aroideana43N3.txt). Type restated: Versteeg 1818 (BO!, lecto,
Hay 1988), Noord [Lorentz, Unir] River, October 1907. History: first
collected by G.M. Versteeg in 1907 (1141 in May, 1818 in October) and by
L.S.A.M. von Römer in 1909 (193, cited by Engler 1911, not located), all on
the Noord River (renamed Lorentz in 1910; now the Unir of the Asmat people);
no further record for over 90 years until R.J. Johns collected it in 1999
(9831, K, Freeport concession main road mile 18–19, 11 March 1999) and 2000
(10638, K, road to Port site, 9 May 2000) on Kew–Bogor–Manokwari expeditions
to the Mimika Regency, an encounter published only as the habit photo that
illustrates the genus on POWO; then Imran photographed it near Timika in 2020
(figs 1–4: habit with brownish-red spathe; blooms and infructescence; bloom;
spadix).

Updated description: massive pachycaul rhizomatous evergreen herb 3–4 m;
rhizome c. 40 cm thick; petioles 1.3–2 m, c. 12 cm thick at the base, plain
green, crowded to scattered small prickles at the base and scattered above,
sheathing in the lower c. 15%, filled with air cavities to 1 cm; blade
ovate-sagittate, held vertical with the posterior lobes down, bullate between
the primary veins, to c. 2.25 × 1.2 m; costae to 3 cm thick; anterior costa
with c. 10 primary laterals; posterior costae naked in the sinus for c. 10 cm,
the basiscopic sides of the posterior lobes overlapping. Blooms solitary, up
to 3 (or more) in close succession separated by foliage leaves; peduncle c.
50 × 4.5 cm, unarmed or with a few prickles; spathe canoe-shaped, erect,
35–50 cm, convolute in the lower c. 8 cm, greenish brown at the base then
burgundy-red to yellowish brown outside, cream/yellow with purple-suffused
streaks within; spadix stipitate 2 cm, 21–30 × 4–5.5 cm, dull brownish pink;
florets bisexual, > 2000 per spadix, c. 3 mm; tepals 4–6, free, 3.5 mm;
stamens 4–6, filaments free, 1 mm, anthers partly exserted at male anthesis;
pistil 4 mm, stigma button-like, ovary unilocular, pluriovulate.
Infructescence massive, deflected, with the marcescent spathe, fruiting
spadix 36–45 × 9–11 cm; berries ellipsoid, 1.5 × 0.6 cm, tips protruding from
the tepals, dark green ripening yellow, 1–5-seeded; seeds reniform,
longitudinally crested, 5–6 mm.

Distribution: Papua Province, Mimika and Asmat Regencies; open swampy areas
with scattered *Metroxylon* and *Campnosperma* (Johns 10638) and disturbed
swamp forest with *Campnosperma*, *Nauclea* and *Pandanus* (Johns 9831), at
10–15 m. Note added in proof: a third gigantic species, *C. timikense*, is
described later in the issue (genus now 14 species, 8 on New Guinea);
"anthuriocarps" (expelled berries hanging on the spadix) are now also known
in *C. hambalii* near Timika.

GBIF holds 17 records (all preserved specimens, 11 georeferenced): Indonesia
12 (R.J. Johns and colleagues, 1990s–2000, Lorentz lowlands) and Papua New
Guinea 5 (Western Province; not cited by Hay & Imran); elevation 10–15 m.
iNaturalist has 3 observations (2025–2026; one wild, two cultivated) with 6
CC BY-NC photos, all copied.

## Distribution and habitat

- **POWO:** New Guinea (native).
- Open swampy areas and disturbed swamp forest of the Mimika and Asmat lowlands (Hay & Imran 2020), and, per GBIF, adjacent Western Province, Papua New Guinea; sea level to c. 15 m.

## Notes

- Flowers 4–6-merous with a pluriovulate ovary and multi-seeded berries are unique in the genus. The berries protrude from the tepals but are not expelled.
- Now in cultivation (iNat, 2025–2026); seed of the other giant, *C. timikense*, is sold commercially, so the group is entering horticulture.

# Cyrtosperma brassii A.Hay

**Status:** accepted (POWO/WCVP).
**Protologue:** Hay, A. (1988). Blumea 33: 439–441, Fig. 5 (drawn from Hay s.n., cult. Lae).
**Type:** L.J. Brass 28282, Papua New Guinea, Milne Bay Province, Rossel Island, Abaleti, 1 October 1956 (L, holotype; A, LAE isotypes). Imaged: L 0041891, A 00025750.
**Diagnosis (Latin):** differs from *C. merkusii* in tetramerous flowers, smooth seeds and leaves rough (asperous) when dried.
**Informal group:** Merkusii group.
**Etymology:** for Leonard J. Brass, collector of the type on the Fifth Archbold Expedition.
**IDs:** IPNI 935715-1 · POWO urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:935715-1 · WCVP 54872 · iNat taxon 937780.

## Description (Hay 1988: 441)

Robust herb to 2.5 m. Leaves 2–several; petioles spreading out from the base,
very prickly with small spines arranged in combs at least towards the base,
mottled green and white; blade sagittate, to 120 cm, held with the anterior
lobe up and the posterior lobes down; costae of the anterior lobe pinnate,
spreading; posterior lobes with the midribs naked in the sinus for c. 6 cm, to
30 cm wide, acuminate; both surfaces asperous in the dry state (not when
fresh). Peduncle somewhat shorter than the petioles. Spathe ovate-lanceolate,
to 18 cm, remaining somewhat constricted above the spadix apex after opening,
boat-shaped, blackish purple, major veins parallel, secondary veins
reticulate; spadix with a c. 5 mm free unarmed stipe, to c. 7 cm, creamy
yellow. Flowers tetramerous; ovaries 1(–2)-ovulate; anthers exserted at male
anthesis. Fruit ovoid, orange-red. Seed smooth, very faintly ridged.

## Distribution and habitat

- **POWO:** New Guinea (native) — Rossel Island, Louisiade Archipelago.
- **Hay 1988:** restricted to Rossel Island, in rainforest. Also Brass 28251 (L), NGF 27090 (L, LAE) and a plant cultivated at Lae. Map: Fig. 3.
- **GBIF:** 6 records (5 specimens, 3 georeferenced), 1956–1982; elevation 50 m.
- **iNaturalist:** none.

## Notes

- One of two Louisiade endemics (with *C. gressittiorum* on Sudest); both are tetramerous members of the Merkusii group, contrasting with the hexamerous *C. merkusii*.
- Was in cultivation at Lae Botanic Gardens in the 1980s.

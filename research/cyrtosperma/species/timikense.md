# Cyrtosperma timikense Imran & A.Hay

**Status:** accepted (POWO/WCVP).
**Protologue:** Hay, A. & Imran (2020). A third enormous New Guinea lasioid — *Cyrtosperma timikense*, a new species from southern Papua Province, Indonesia. Aroideana 43(3–4): 97–107, figs 1–7. Read 2026-09-07 from the Drive text export of Aroideana 43(3–4) (`LITERATURE`, Aroideana43N3.txt).
**Type:** Imran s.n., 28 October 2020, Indonesia, Papua Province, Mimika Regency, near Timika, 4°39'47"S 136°49'36"E (MAN, holo; iso BO, to be distributed).
**Informal group:** by the authors' comparison, with *C. cuspidispathum* (long-attenuate, basally convolute spathe; very numerous small prickles).
**Etymology:** from Timika, capital of the Mimika Regency, near which it was found.
**IDs:** IPNI 77214865-1 · POWO urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:77214865-1 · WCVP 546918 · WFO wfo-1000022045.

## The protologue (Hay & Imran 2020)

Diagnosis: resembles *C. cuspidispathum* in the very elongate spathe, gigantic size and small prickles; differs in the strongly clumping habit, the somewhat angular distal petiole and geniculum, the much narrower spathe with a somewhat inflated basal green portion, the stamens exserted at male anthesis, the fruits apparently not expelled from the spadix, and the rather elaborately crested seeds, two per berry.

Description: massive, evergreen, strongly clump-forming herb to c. 3 m; rhizome condensed, subterranean, 25–40 cm diam. with the leaf bases; leaves c. 8 per crown. Petioles 2–2.5 m, vertical and close together in the lower half then weakly spreading, sheathing in the lower 30–40 cm (sheath wings papery, degrading to fibres), dark grey-green densely obliquely streaked buff below, bright green and somewhat angular in section above, sparsely armed with very small obliquely down-pointing prickles (following the streaks below; scattered and upturned above), geniculate for c. 7 cm. Blades broadly triangular-sagittate, 1–1.5 m, unarmed, relatively small, anterior lobe up and posterior lobes down; anterior lobe 40–50 × 50–60 cm; posterior lobes 60–90 cm; posterior costae diverging at c. 110°, naked in the sinus for c. 8 cm; anterior costa with c. 9 primary lateral veins each side at 60–70°. Blooms solitary to three in succession; peduncles 1.5–2.3 m, densely prickly throughout. Spathe to c. 80 cm, convolute, green and slightly inflated in the basal 7–11 cm, then narrowly canoe-shaped and very long-attenuate, dull purple, faintly streaked, marcescent. Spadix stipitate 2–3 cm (stipe minutely prickly), tapering-cylindric, c. 20 cm at female anthesis, c. 26 cm at male anthesis; florets with 6 free tepals and 6 stamens; anthers exserted at male anthesis; pistil unilocular(?), bi-ovulate(?); stigma discoid, sessile. Infructescence erect then deflected, to c. 40 cm; berries red-orange, depressed-globose, weakly lobed, apparently not expelled, mostly 2-seeded with one seed larger; seeds reniform with prominently lobed crests, c. 0.5 or c. 1 cm.

Distribution and habitat: Papua Province, vicinity of Timika, known only from the type population; open degraded swamp forest, c. 8 m.

Notes in the paper: full comparison with *C. cuspidispathum* (solitary vs clumping; spathe 1.25 m vs 80 cm; vase-shaped purple-brown lower spathe vs small inflated green base; yellowish main veins vs indistinct streaks; anthers not exserted vs exserted; rotund expelled berries vs depressed-globose retained berries; barely ornamented vs crested seeds). The species lies well outside the range of *C. cuspidispathum*: the westernmost southern record of that species is c. 500 km east of Timika, and the Jayapura records are across the Central Cordillera. All seven figures are from the type population (photos Imran).

## What the other sources say

- **Hay & Imran (2020, giganteum paper, note added in proof):** announces the discovery; the genus then at 14 species, 8 on New Guinea.
- **POWO:** native to W. New Guinea.
- **Hariri et al. (2024)** cite it in their count of 14 species (before *C. prasinispathum*).
- **GBIF:** no records. **iNaturalist:** none identified. Seed is offered commercially (e.g. onszaden.com lists "Cyrtosperma timikense"), so cultivated plants exist.

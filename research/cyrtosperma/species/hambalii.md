# Cyrtosperma hambalii A.Dearden & A.Hay

**Status:** accepted (POWO/WCVP).
**Protologue:** Dearden, A. & Hay, A. (2001). A new species of *Cyrtosperma* (Araceae) from West Papua. Aroideana 24: 102–104, figs 1–2. Read 2026-09-07 from the Drive text export of Aroideana 24 (`LITERATURE`, aroideana24.txt).
**Type:** A. Hay & A. Dearden s.n., 12 May 2001, cult. Redlynch, Cairns, Queensland, ex Indonesia, West Papua, Timika, Freeport Nursery, brought in from the vicinity of Timika (orig. coll. G.G. Hambali s.n.) (NSW, holo).
**Informal group:** not assigned; the authors compare it with *C. beccarianum* (leaf shape), *C. cuspidispathum* (long spathe) and *C. carrii* (spathe colour, exserted stamens).
**Etymology:** for Gregory G. Hambali, "in acknowledgement of his long and expert contribution to Indonesian botany and horticulture" and his part in the rediscovery of *Lasia concinna* (Hambali & Sizemore 1997).
**IDs:** IPNI 20001020-1 · POWO urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:20001020-1 · WCVP 54883 · WFO wfo-0000936214 · iNat taxon 937776.

## The protologue (Dearden & Hay 2001)

Latin diagnosis: differs from *C. beccarianum* in the shorter petiole, the very long spathe and the exserted stamens; from *C. cuspidispathum* in the much smaller plant, the shorter petiole, the very small anterior lobe of the leaf, the wholly erect spathe, the much shorter spadix and the exserted stamens.

Description (English in the original): rhizomatous herb to c. 1 m; stem condensed, subterranean; leaves c. 4 together; petiole erect, short, about half the blade, c. 17 cm, geniculate in the apical 1 cm, unarmed, dark olive brown below, mid green above, olive green mottled olive brown between, sheathing in the lower third; blade sagittate, c. 32 cm, held with the posterior lobes erect and the anterior lobe pointing obliquely down; anterior lobe narrowly triangular, 8 × c. 2.5 cm; posterior lobes rather narrowly elliptic, c. 23 × 7 cm, acuminate for 2 cm; anterior costa without primary venation; posterior costae naked in the sinus for 5–8 mm, with 5–6 primary lateral veins each side at c. 30°. Inflorescence solitary; peduncle 47 cm, much exceeding the petioles, prickly only in the distal 12 cm. Spathe erect, unarmed, with a prominent abaxial central ridge, 53 × 2.2 cm, convolute at the base, dark greenish brown with eight green vertical stripes outside, blackish inside. Spadix sessile, cylindric, blunt, 6 × 0.8 cm; flowers hexamerous, c. 1.5 mm; anthers exserted at male anthesis. Fruit unknown.

Distribution: endemic to New Guinea, known only from the vicinity of Timika, southern West Papua. Habitat: not recorded.

Notes in the paper: "strikingly elegant plant with its 'rabbit's ears' leaves and slender perfectly erect inflorescence held high above them"; the sheath reaching a third of the petiole is unusual in the genus. Correction of Hay (1988): the stamens of *C. carrii* are exserted at anthesis (the 1988 statement rested on limited herbarium material). Key: new couplet 3a (anterior lobe much reduced, narrowly triangular; posterior lobes erect) → *C. hambalii*; 3b → 4 (= 3a of Hay 1988). The genus then stood at 12 species. ## What the other sources say

- **Hay & Imran (2020, Aroideana 43: 11, note added in proof):** expelled berries hanging on the spadix (the "anthuriocarp" of *C. cuspidispathum*) have been observed by Imran in *C. hambalii* near Timika — so fruit is now known to exist, and the character is not unique to *C. cuspidispathum*.
- **POWO:** native to SW New Guinea.
- **Hariri et al. (2024, Webbia)** — the *C. prasinispathum* paper: living plants at Bogor Botanic Gardens collected in Sorong (Southwest Papua) in 2007 had been labelled *C. hambalii*; on examination they proved to be a different, new species. Their Fig. 1J shows a *C. hambalii* leaf for comparison.
- **iNaturalist:** 6 observations (2023–2026), all flagged wild, 4 research grade: Mimika (2) and Sorong (4); 16 photos, 14 copied (CC BY-NC-SA 10, CC BY-NC 4); 2 all-rights-reserved listed only. The Sorong records deserve a check against *C. prasinispathum*.
- **GBIF:** 1 record (a 2025 human observation, Papua Barat); no specimens indexed.

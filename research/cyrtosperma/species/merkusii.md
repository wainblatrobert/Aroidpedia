# Cyrtosperma merkusii (Hassk.) Schott

**Status:** accepted (POWO/WCVP). Type species of the genus by synonymy (*C. lasioides* Griff.).
**Protologue:** Schott, H.W. (1857). Oesterr. Bot. Wochenbl. 7: 61 ("Aroideen-Skizzen").
**Basionym:** *Lasia merkusii* Hassk., Cat. Hort. Bot. Bogor. alter: 59 (1844); Pl. Jav. Rar.: 161 (1848). Type: Hasskarl s.n., Java, cult. Hort. Bogor (L, holotype). IPNI's original-remarks field for Schott's combination reads "Sumatra".
**Informal group:** Merkusii group.
**IDs:** IPNI 86826-1 · POWO urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:86826-1 · WCVP 54892 · WFO wfo-0000936224 · GBIF 206541486 · iNat taxon 210278.

## Original description

**Held:** Schott 1857, Oesterr. Bot. Wochenbl. 7: 61–62 (`Schott-1857-Oesterr-Bot-Wochenbl-7`, BHL page 29952872, reached through BHL's OpenURL resolver). Under the heading "Aroideae. Skizzirt von H. W. Schott", Schott writes that the plant Hasskarl first made known and placed in *Lasia* (Cat. Hort. Bogor. 1844: 59) agrees entirely, in habit and in flower and fruit structure, with Griffith's later *Cyrtosperma* (Not. 3: 149–150, 1851); he gives a short generic character (spathe scarcely twisted, at length marcescent-persistent; spadix elongate; flowers hexamerous; berries smooth; seed reniform, margined-crested; albumen fleshy; leaves sagittate), contrasts *Lasia*, and separates the two species: *C. lasioides* Griff. — blade sagittate, long cuspidate-acuminate, basal lobes parted by a narrow sinus, spathe 3–6 inches, spadix reaching the middle of the spathe; *C. Merkusii* S. — blade sagittate, cuspidate-acuminate, basal lobes parted by a wide sinus, spathe 10–12 inches, spadix elongate, two-thirds of the spathe.

## Description (after Hay 1988: 432–433)

Robust to gigantic herb to 4 m. Rhizome short and slender to very large,
sometimes globose and weighing up to 70 kg, weakly to freely suckering.
Leaves several, sagittate, rarely hastate; petioles c. 40 cm to c. 3 m,
heavily armed (often very heavily, with stout conical spines towards the
base) to unarmed in cultivars; blade c. 30 cm to 1.3 m long, to c. 80 cm
wide, armed or not beneath, held horizontally to vertically with the
posterior lobes down; posterior lobes somewhat exceeding the anterior;
posterior midribs naked in the sinus for c. 5 cm. Peduncle like the petiole
but usually shorter, its geniculus blackening on drying. Spathe very variable,
2.5 to c. 30 cm, white, yellow, green or purple, pale forms usually purple-
streaked outside, rolled back to deflexed to erect with increasing size, the
erect ones constricted above the spadix apex; spadix 2–24 cm, at least half
the spathe, sessile to stipitate. Flowers hexamerous; ovary (1–)2-ovulate;
stamens exserted at male anthesis. Fruit reddish orange, sessile,
1(–2)-seeded. Seed campylotropous, almost circular to shallowly kidney-shaped,
5–11 mm, brown, with c. 3 raised warty longitudinal crests to sparsely warty
and faintly crested.

Hay notes that spathe poise follows its size relative to the spadix (small
spathes are stretched and deflected, large ones stand erect), that wild plants
are heavily armed throughout the range while the unarmed, massive forms are
cultivated selections, and that cultivated plants have larger, less ornamented,
less curved seeds and lower fruit set. Nicolson (1979) suggested surveying the
cultivars for polyploidy.

## Distribution and habitat

- **POWO native range:** Borneo, Caroline Is., Cook Is., Fiji, Gilbert Is., Java, Marshall Is., New Guinea, Peninsular Malaysia, Philippines, Samoa, Santa Cruz Is., Society Is., Solomon Is., Sumatra, Vanuatu. **Introduced:** Line Is., Marquesas, Nauru, Tokelau-Manihiki, Tuamotu, Tuvalu, Vietnam, Wallis-Futuna Is.
- **Hay 1988:** Malesia to Oceania, excluding Sulawesi, the Moluccas and mainland New Guinea; lowland swamp forest, open swamps and cultivation. Hay saw no wild material from mainland New Guinea (the Kiriwina and Manus collections are cultivated plants); POWO nevertheless lists New Guinea as native. Wild and "clearly little used" in Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra, Borneo and (very rarely) Java; Philippine plants are the unarmed cultivated kind, perhaps escaped; both wild-type and cultivated giants occur in the Solomons.
- **Since 1988:** Athens & Stevenson (2011) recovered its pollen from early-Holocene, pre-human mangrove cores on Pohnpei, so the natural range was wider than the wild collections suggest. The Sangihe–Talaud–Siau islands off North Sulawesi grow it as *daluga* (Erlinawati et al. 2018), a region Hay listed as blank.
- **GBIF (1,086-record genus pull):** 623 records, 581 preserved specimens, 146 georeferenced, 1837–2026. Countries: Malaysia 80, Marshall Is. 70, Micronesia 60, Indonesia 53, Philippines 39, Fiji 39, Palau 32, French Polynesia 28, Cook Is. 25, Kiribati 20, Singapore 19, PNG 16. Elevation 0–1,792 m, median 10 m. 218 records still labelled *C. chamissonis*, 19 *C. lasioides*, 12 *C. edule*.
- **iNaturalist:** 110 observations (2008–2026), 72 flagged wild and 38 cultivated, 46 research grade; Micronesia, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, Palau, Kiribati. 209 photos, 150 copied (CC BY-NC 115, CC BY 18, CC BY-NC-SA 8, CC0 6, CC BY-SA 4); 58 all-rights-reserved photos listed only.

## Notes

- **The crop.** Giant swamp taro; babai (Kiribati), pulaka (Tuvalu), baba (Guam), ape veo (Raiatea), daluga (Sangihe), sampi gede (Sundanese). Hay (1988: 433–434) summarises the economic botany (Barrau; Burkill 1935; Brown 1931; Parham; Purseglove 1972; Chandra 1984) and argues the species "deserves further biological inquiry" as an early Melanesian and Polynesian crop, while noting that by 1988 it was giving way to *Colocasia* and *Xanthosoma*. Its domestication history is unresolved: either a natural split range (West Malesia / Papuasia) with independent domestication, or the Solomon wild-type plants are relics of early spiny introductions from the west.
- **Cultivation.** Pit cultivation on atolls; heavily armed wild forms; cultivars informally named. Recent Indonesian work on corm starch, flour and nutrition is listed in `bibliography.md` (mostly not downloaded).
- **Hasskarl's plant.** Koorders (1919) could find no authentic material of *Lasia merkusii* at Bogor and equated his Bantam plant (his ms. name *bantamensis*) with it on the strength of Hasskarl's description and the shared Sundanese name Sampi.

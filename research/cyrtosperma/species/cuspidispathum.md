# Cyrtosperma cuspidispathum Alderw.

**Status:** accepted (POWO/WCVP).
**Protologue:** van Alderwerelt van Rosenburgh, C.R.W.K. (1920). New or noteworthy Malayan Araceae II. Bull. Jard. Bot. Buitenzorg sér. 3, 1: 374 (IPNI and WCVP cite p. 371; the description is on p. 374 of the scan, as Hay cites). Also Krause & Alderwerelt, Nova Guinea 14: 215 (1924).
**Type:** Gjellerup 63, Irian Jaya [Papua], Eta River, Upper Tami, 2 April 1910, "shady clayey river side; alt. 85 m" (B?, holotype, not seen by Hay; L). Imaged: L 0041893 (isotype).
**Informal group:** Cuspidispathum group (with *C. kokodense*).
**IDs:** IPNI 86814-1 · POWO urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:86814-1 · WCVP 54877 · GBIF 206541571 · iNat taxon 937779.

## Names

Misapplied names that belong here (Hay 1988: 443): Engler's New Guinea
*C. merkusii* (Monogr. Phan. 1879; Pflanzenreich 1911 pro parte, Schlechter
14313, 16389, 20363, Hollrung 793, Lauterbach 75; Engler & Krause 1912 as to
Gjellerup 63) and *C. edule* (as to Hollrung 602, Lauterbach 956); Krause &
Alderwerelt's *C. macrotum* (1924) as to specimens cited. Engler's
Pflanzenreich fig. 6, captioned *C. edule*, "is clearly *C. cuspidispathum*".

## Original description (Alderwerelt 1920: 374, held)

Latin diagnosis, summarised: petiole c. 75 cm, dark olive- and purple-flamed,
sparsely and shortly prickly; blade hastate or sagittate-hastate, c. 50 cm,
anterior lobe oblong c. 20 × 7 cm with an abrupt slender beak, posterior lobes
obliquely sublanceolate c. 30 × 10 cm separated by a right-angled sinus,
posterior costae naked in the sinus for 0.5–1 cm, unarmed beneath; peduncle c.
85 cm, sparsely prickly below, rather copiously above; spathe c. 35 cm, dark
violet, sparsely veined, the lower part long-oblong c. 18 × 4 cm abruptly
narrowed into a linear, subulate cusp about as long as the lower part; spadix
stipitate (stipe c. 1 cm), cylindric, c. 7.5 cm × 6 mm, greenish white;
tepals and stamens 6; ovaries cylindric, flat-based, truncate, 1-ovulate. The
epithet refers to the cuspidate spathe.

## Description (after Hay 1988: 443)

Moderately robust to gigantic, solitary or rarely suckering herb to c. 4 m;
rhizome short, creeping, subterranean, to 13 cm diameter, the older parts
rotting quickly. Leaves 2–several; petiole brown, green or mottled with white;
armature sparse to dense, the spines (at least low down) small, squat,
abruptly upturned and easily dislodged; petioles standing subparallel. Blade
hastate or sagittate, to 1.3 m, in smaller plants with lobes much narrower
than long, held with the posterior lobes down and the anterior lobe up,
somewhat flaccid, unarmed. Peduncle somewhat shorter than the petioles.
Spathe 17 to c. 100 cm, clasping in the lower quarter, ovate-lanceolate, erect
in small plants, in large ones the acuminate apical portion greatly elongated,
spirally twisted and dangling; purple-brown, usually yellow-veined outside,
with pale pink to cream streaks towards the midline within; spadix to 18 cm in
flower, with a free, sometimes armed stipe 2–6 cm. Flowers hexamerous;
anthers not exceeding the tepals at male anthesis; stigmas more or less
sessile, white turning brown; ovaries predominantly uniovulate. Fruit expelled
from the spadix when ripe and held there by the stripped adaxial epidermis of
the tepals; seed slightly and irregularly ridged.

## Distribution and habitat

- **POWO:** New Guinea (native).
- **Hay 1988:** Irian Jaya (Jayapura: Eta River) and Papua New Guinea (West and East Sepik, Madang, Western, Southern Highlands and Gulf Provinces); lowland rainforest undergrowth and regrowth, predominantly on alluvial soils; Jacobs 9187 from lower montane forest on limestone at 500–600 m. Map: Hay Fig. 8.
- **GBIF:** 59 records (52 specimens, 26 georeferenced), 1910–2023; Papua New Guinea 44, Indonesia 5; elevation 24–758 m, median 305 m. Takeuchi, van Veldhuizen, Womersley & Vink among collectors.
- **iNaturalist:** 10 observations (2018–2026), 7 wild and 3 cultivated, 3 research grade, Indonesian Papua; 24 photos, all copied (CC BY-NC 19, CC BY 4, CC BY-NC-SA 1).

## Ecology and reproductive biology (Hay 1988: 443–447)

- A regrowth species: in the Gogol Valley (Madang) plants in logged regrowth reached the maximum recorded size seven years after clearing, and seedlings established in one-year-old regrowth. Cultivated at Lae the rhizomes sucker into large clumps, not seen in the wild.
- Stigmas become receptive shortly after the spathe unrolls, in a rapid basipetal sequence over about two days, and stay receptive with a nectar drop for about three weeks unless pollinated. Pollen is released only after the stigmas cease; anthers open by apical pore-like slits and extrude pollen in coherent rope-like masses, dehiscence simultaneous within a flower and slowly basipetal within an inflorescence over several weeks; the larger the inflorescence, the longer the anthesis.
- Beyond pollen there is no reward once the stigmas stop; weevils *Trochorhopalus strangulatus* Gyll. (det. W. Gagné, BISH) feed on tissue inside the spathe base. The inflorescence emits a faint musty fruity odour day and night before, during and after anthesis.
- The geniculus turns the spadix from pointing up to pointing straight down as fruit develops. Ripe fruits are pushed out of the spadix and dangle by the tepal epidermis "recalling arillate seeds dangling by funicles" — otherwise known in the genus perhaps only in *C. kokodense*, and a feature common in *Anthurium*.

## Notes

- One of the giants of the genus; Hay & Imran (2020) call *C. timikense* "a third enormous New Guinea lasioid" after this species and *C. giganteum*, and compare it with *C. cuspidispathum*.
- Frequently in cultivation in Indonesia and among collectors (iNat cultivated records).

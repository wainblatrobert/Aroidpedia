# Cyrtosperma Griff.

**Protologue:** Griffith, W. (1851). Notulae ad Plantas Asiaticas 3: 149; Icones Plantarum Asiaticarum t. 169 (C. lasioides).
**Type species:** *Cyrtosperma lasioides* Griff. (= *C. merkusii*).
**Synonym:** *Arisacontis* Schott, Bonplandia 5: 129 (1857); type *A. chamissonis* Schott (= *C. merkusii*).
**Placement:** Araceae, subfamily Lasioideae, tribe Lasieae, subtribe Lasiinae (Hay 1988). Site tree: Lasioideae / Lasieae.
**Accepted species (POWO/WCVP, June 2026 release):** 15. Hay's 1988 monograph had 11; four have been added since (*C. hambalii* 2001, *C. timikense* 2020, *C. hayii* 2024, *C. prasinispathum* 2024).
**Etymology:** Greek *kyrtos*, curved, and *sperma*, seed: the strongly curved (campylotropous) seed. Schott (1858: 84) spells it out: "Denominatio composita ex κυρτός curvus et σπέρμα semen".

## Circumscription (after Hay 1988: 428–429)

Massive to slender, usually solitary, occasionally clump-forming rhizomatous
herbs. Rhizome thick, with condensed internodes, creeping, the older parts
long persistent or quickly rotting. Leaves several together on spiny, usually
mottled petioles up to c. 3 m long, with a prominent pulvinar geniculum at the
apex; blades hastate to sagittate, generally all held facing the same way,
posterior lobes longer than the anterior. Inflorescence solitary, rarely
paired, on a peduncle like the petiole. Spathe erect, rarely hood-forming,
sometimes with the upper part long-acuminate and spirally twisted, convolute
or not in the lower part, blackish purple to white; spadix sessile to
stipitate. Flowers hermaphrodite throughout the spadix, 6-(5-)4-tepalate and
-staminate; filaments free; receptive stigmas wet; ovary unilocular, multi- to
uniovulate, placentation basal to basal/parietal. Fruit a red or orange
berry, in one species expelled from the spadix and left dangling by the inner
epidermis of the tepals (as in *Anthurium*). Seed strongly campylotropous,
reniform to orbicular to helically twisted, pachychalazal, strophiolate,
smooth, crested or warty, albuminous.

Hay's generic key separates *Cyrtosperma* from its Old World allies thus:
*Lasia* has suffruticose spiny-internoded stems, often pinnatifid leaves, a
caducous spathe and apical placentation; *Lasiomorpha* (Africa) has angular
petioles armed in rows, stoloniferous colonies, a persistent spathe and
united filaments; *Podolasia* (West Malesia) has spines mixed straight and
down-turned, a rhizome with distinct internodes and a large smooth orbicular
seed; *Cyrtosperma* has spines straight and up-turned or all up-turned, a
condensed rhizome, and a crested and/or warty seed (if smooth then under 5
mm). Note that *C. hayii* (2024) is described as having downward-pointing
spines, so that key character is no longer absolute.

## Distribution

Malesia and Oceania. One species, *C. merkusii*, ranges from the Malay
Peninsula, Sumatra, Borneo, Java and the Philippines across the Pacific (in
cultivation as the giant swamp taro as far as the Society and Marquesas
Islands); every other species is Papuasian: New Guinea and its satellite
islands (Aru, Batanta, Louisiades, d'Entrecasteaux), Bougainville and the
Solomon Islands. Hay (1988) recorded none from Sulawesi, Halmahera or
Australia. It is, in Hay's words, "the only oligotypic genus of Araceae more
diverse east than west of Wallace's Line".

The genus's centre is New Guinea, where the species are terrestrial herbs of
lowland swamp forest, rainforest undergrowth, regrowth and stream sides,
mostly below 700 m; *C. bougainvillense* is montane (600–2000 m).

## Informal species groups (Hay 1988: 429–430)

Hay declined to erect formal sections but used four informal groups, which
Hariri et al. (2024) continue to use:

- **Merkusii group** — stamens exserted from the tepals at male anthesis;
  throughout the range. *C. merkusii*, *C. johnstonii*, *C. gressittiorum*,
  *C. brassii*, *C. bougainvillense*, and tentatively *C. giganteum*.
- **Cuspidispathum group** — stamens not exserted; upper spathe twisted
  through at least a full turn or lower spathe convolute with hexamerous
  flowers. *C. cuspidispathum*, *C. kokodense*. (*C. timikense*, 2020, "most
  resembles *C. cuspidispathum*".)
- **Carrii group** — lower spathe convolute, flowers tetramerous. *C. carrii*.
- **Macrotum group** — lower spathe not convolute, flowers hexa-, penta- or
  tetramerous. *C. macrotum*, *C. beccarianum*, and (Hariri et al. 2024)
  *C. hayii* and *C. prasinispathum*.

## Nomenclatural history in brief

Griffith described the genus in 1851 on Malayan *C. lasioides*. Schott
(1857, Bonplandia) reviewed the "Lasinae", transferred Hasskarl's Javanese
*Lasia merkusii* (1844) to *Cyrtosperma*, and erected *Arisacontis* for a
Chamisso leaf from the Marshall Islands. Engler (1879 in de Candolle's
Monographiae; 1911 in Das Pflanzenreich) built a broad pantropical
*Cyrtosperma* on the unilocular ovary, taking in African and South American
plants. Alderwerelt van Rosenburgh (1920) and Krause (1924) added five New
Guinea names. Hay (1988) dismantled Engler's concept: the American and
African species went to *Dracontium*, *Anaphyllopsis*, *Urospatha* and
*Lasiomorpha*, *C. angustilobum* proved to be a seedling of *Podolasia
stipitata*, four of the Alderwerelt/Krause names were sunk into a broadly
variable *C. macrotum*, the Pacific crop names (*C. chamissonis*, *C. edule*)
and the type species *C. lasioides* were sunk into *C. merkusii*, and six new
species were described from New Guinea and the Solomons. Since then Dearden &
Hay (2001), Hay & Imran (2020), Raynalta et al. (2024) and Hariri et al.
(2024) have added one species each from Indonesian New Guinea.

## Biology (Hay 1988)

- Flowers are protogynous and the species studied are self-sterile;
  *C. johnstonii*, which fails to set seed in cultivation, is probably a single
  clone (Hay 1988: 439), a point Hay (2020) revisits.
- In *C. cuspidispathum* the stigmas become receptive in a rapid basipetal
  sequence over about two days and stay receptive, each bearing a nectar
  drop, for about three weeks unless pollinated; pollen is released only
  afterwards, from apical pore-like slits, in coherent rope-like masses, in a
  slow basipetal sequence over several weeks. The inflorescence smells faintly
  musty-fruity day and night. Weevils (*Trochorhopalus strangulatus*) feed on
  the tissue inside the spathe base (Hay 1988: 446).
- The geniculus at the top of the peduncle reorients the spadix as it fruits:
  to pointing straight down in *C. cuspidispathum*, to horizontal in
  *C. macrotum*.
- Ripe fruits are expelled from the spadix and hang by the stripped tepal
  epidermis in *C. cuspidispathum* (and perhaps *C. kokodense*), a mechanism
  otherwise known in *Anthurium*.
- Chromosomes: *C. johnstonii* 2n = 26 (Marchant 1973, cited by Hay).
- Seeds carry the characters Hay used most: helically twisted and stony in
  *C. bougainvillense*, crested in *C. merkusii*, smooth in the Macrotum
  and Carrii groups.

## Ethnobotany

*C. merkusii* is the giant swamp taro of the Pacific (babai in Kiribati,
pulaka in Tuvalu, baba on Guam, ape veo on Raiatea, daluga/dalugha in the
Sangihe–Talaud islands, sampi gede in Sundanese Java), grown in dug pits and
swamps for its enormous corm. Hay (1988: 433–434) reviews the literature
(Barrau, Burkill 1935, Chandra 1984) and the unresolved question of where it
was domesticated. Athens & Stevenson (2011) found its pollen in pre-human
sediments on Pohnpei, so its natural range was wider than the wild
collections suggest. Recent agronomic and genetic work centres on North
Sulawesi (Erlinawati et al. 2018 and the Indonesian literature listed in
`bibliography.md`). *C. johnstonii* is a long-cultivated ornamental and a
Thai traditional medicine now studied for its flavonoids (Naksuriya et al.
2022).
